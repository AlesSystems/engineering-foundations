# Study Method

## Optimize for durable understanding

Reading produces familiarity; explanation and experiment expose understanding. Every major topic should leave evidence that another engineer can inspect and challenge.

## The loop

1. **Question:** begin with two or three questions you genuinely want answered.
2. **Predict:** write your current model before consulting sources.
3. **Study:** prefer primary sources and focused sections over exhaustive consumption.
4. **Experiment:** make the system reveal its behavior through code, tracing, measurement, or failure injection.
5. **Explain:** rewrite the model in plain language and add a diagram.
6. **Retrieve:** revisit the questions without notes after a few days.
7. **Revise:** record misconceptions and remaining uncertainty explicitly.

## Source discipline

- Prefer specifications, official documentation, textbooks, course material, source code, and original papers.
- Separate observed behavior from interpretation.
- Link the exact source and section when possible; include an access date for mutable web material.
- Quote sparingly. Synthesize in your own words.
- Verify AI-generated claims before publishing them.

## A relaxed cadence

- Sessions may move across calendar weeks.
- Do not create a growing “catch-up” burden.
- Reduce scope before sacrificing the experiment or explanation.
- Stop a topic when you can explain its core model and name the next depth question.

## Review checkpoints

At the end of each four-week block, answer from memory:

- What are the central abstractions?
- Where are the protection or trust boundaries?
- Which trade-offs recur?
- What failure modes surprised me?
- How does this connect to my daily engineering work?
